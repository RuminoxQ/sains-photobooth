Camera

Built-in, USB, capture-card and phone-as-webcam cameras all appear here.

Detected cameras

    Plug a camera in at any time — in Auto mode the booth switches to it on its own. A camera that’s open in another app (Zoom, Teams, the camera’s own software) can’t be used here until that app lets go of it.

    Fast mode keeps the live view smooth and makes photos appear instantly: camera capped at 1080p, quicker clean-up and a lighter GIF.

    Automatic finds the camera’s highest resolution (4K or more on cameras that offer it). The live status above shows what the camera actually gave. Choose 1080p only if the live view feels laggy on an older computer.

    Photo enhance

    Sharpens detail and lifts flat or dim lighting on every photo after it’s taken. Skips fine noise so skin stays smooth. Affects prints, downloads and the GIF’s final frame.

    Grain reduction

    Blends several frames at the moment of the photo, then smooths leftover grain while keeping edges sharp. Use Strong in dim rooms or with basic webcams.

    Turn off if there is text in the background you want readable.

    Shooting
    Countdown
    Pause between photos

    When more photos are taken than the layout needs, guests tap their favourites. A layout never takes fewer than it needs.

    Films the countdown before each photo (3, 5 or 10 seconds — set under Countdown), then freezes on the photo. Guests get their whole layout as a moving GIF to scan or save alongside the print.

    When the GIF ends

    Either way the movement ends by freezing on the actual photo (held 2 seconds before any replay).

    GIF size

    “Same as photo” makes the GIF the full print size (e.g. 600×1800 for the classic strip) — sharpest, but bigger files that take longer to upload. “Smaller file” is half size and quicker on slow venue Wi-Fi.

    Format

    GIF plays anywhere, including chats. Video is sharper and smaller but needs a video player.

    Take-home options

    Prints each strip twice on one 4R (4×6″) sheet so you can cut it in half — portrait strips side by side, landscape strips one above the other. Off prints a single 2×6″ page.

    Uploads the finished print to your own storage and shows a QR code that opens the file directly — no download pages, no ads. Needs an internet connection.

    QR codes on the print

    None, one code in the bottom-left or bottom-right corner, or both codes (PHOTO left, GIF right). The take-home screen always shows both codes. Printing waits for the upload.

    The single code opens

    Used when the print has one code. Without a GIF it always opens the photo.

    When guests scan

    “Save to gallery” opens the photo or GIF on the guest’s phone and shows them how to keep it: press and hold › Save to Photos on iPhone, or › Download image on Android. GIFs stay animated. “Download file” saves straight to the phone’s Downloads (iPhone puts it in the Files app, not Photos).

    Shown on your Cloudinary dashboard after you sign up free at cloudinary.com.

    In Cloudinary: Settings › Upload › Upload presets › Add, set Signing mode: Unsigned, save, and paste its name here. Guests get a direct res.cloudinary.com link to their photo or video.

    Get one free at api.imgbb.com. The QR opens the image file directly (i.ibb.co). ImgBB can’t host videos. Stored only in this browser.

    Receives a POST with the image as a form file. Must allow cross-origin requests.

    Where the photo link sits in the JSON reply, e.g. url or data.link. A plain-text reply is used as-is.

    Save folder
    Where photos are saved

    No folder chosen — files go to Downloads.

    Saves the finished print (and the GIF when it’s on) after every session — to the save folder, or to Downloads when no folder is chosen.

    Each photo on its own, full camera quality, without the frame — handy for an event album.

    Puts files in a dated subfolder, e.g. 2026-11-16.

    One-tap printing
    Print without the print window

    Print then goes straight to the computer’s default printer — no dialog for guests. Chrome only allows this when it’s opened in “kiosk printing” mode, so it’s switched on outside the booth, once per laptop:

    1. Make your photo printer the computer’s default printer, with 4×6″ paper, borderless and glossy photo paper as its defaults.
    2. Quit Chrome completely (⌘+Q on a Mac).
    3. Mac: copy the command, open Terminal, paste it and press Return. Windows: download the launcher and double-click it. Use this every time you open the booth.
